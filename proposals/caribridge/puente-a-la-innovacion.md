## Problem statement

Puerto Rico built one sustained economic expansion in the 20th century, Operation
Bootstrap (Manos a la Obra), by relying on a single federal tax provision
(Section 936). When Congress phased that provision out between 1996 and 2006,
manufacturing's share of the economy fell and no replacement growth engine was
put in place. The proposal argues Puerto Rico can build a second engine now,
one based on research capacity, capital formation, and transparent
institutions rather than on any single federal provision that could again be
withdrawn.

## Proposed mechanism

A five-pillar domestic plan, sequenced so earlier pillars build credibility
for later ones:

1. Advance the Puerto Rico Science, Technology and Research Trust's NSF
   Regional Innovation Engines award toward Type 2 status.
2. Close the later-stage capital gap behind Parallel18 and Grupo Guayacán's
   seed-stage work, via a follow-on fund and a diaspora co-investment vehicle.
3. Prioritize one completed, publicly tracked grid-reliability project over
   investment marketing.
4. Build open contracting, a public expenditure dashboard, and independent
   audit authority into any new capital pool from the start.
5. Use Puerto Rico's bicultural, bilingual, U.S.-integrated position to
   develop AI systems that facilitate value and capital flows between the
   U.S. and Latin America/Caribbean markets in both directions.

A September 2026 addendum, "CariBridge," extends this into a trade framework
adapted from Intermestic Partners' SouthBridge concept, built on Puerto Rico's
existing Foreign-Trade Zones (FTZ 7, 61, 163), ports (San Juan, Ponce,
Mayagüez), and airports (SJU, BQN), rather than proposing new institutions.

## Risks / open constraints

The source document names these explicitly rather than glossing over them,
and this repo should treat them the same way:

- Puerto Rico cannot independently negotiate trade agreements; treaty and
  trade-agreement authority rests with the U.S. federal government.
- The Jones Act (Merchant Marine Act of 1920) still governs mainland-to-PR
  coastwise shipping; this is a live, contested federal policy debate, not
  a settled issue, and the proposal does not claim otherwise.
- CBP retains customs jurisdiction; any "single-window" customs proposal
  would sit on top of federal systems, not replace them.
- Puerto Rico holds CARICOM observer status only, not full membership.
- Any new public spending would still need to satisfy FOMB fiscal-plan
  review under PROMESA.
- The document is written and published by an interested party (GENIA
  Americas), not a neutral third party, and its Investment Thesis section
  explicitly states it is not financial, legal, or investment advice.
- Specific figures cited (NSF award amount, population decline percentage,
  GAO report number) have not been independently verified by this repo and
  should be checked against primary sources before being restated elsewhere
  in this project.
